Study in Italy

Study in Italy for Indian students

Degrees from some of the world's oldest universities, with public fees that can be a fraction of other destinations. The catch is the paperwork. That is where we come in.

  • #87Politecnico di Milano, QS 2027
  • 20 h/weekstudent work limit

Photo: Piazza Maggiore, Bologna · Ingo Mehling · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Italy rewards students who plan early. Universities publish their own deadlines, foreign students pre-enrol on a national portal called Universitaly, and the visa checklist differs slightly from one consulate to the next. Get the order right and Italy can be one of the best-value routes into Europe.

Below is what we tell every family who asks us about Italy: what it costs, how scholarships really work, and the steps in the order they happen.

Why students choose Italy

Serious universities

Politecnico di Milano ranks 87th in the QS World University Rankings 2027 and is the highest-ranked university in Italy. Sapienza (111th) and Bologna (123rd) follow.

Fees that vary, and can be low

Public universities set fees by programme and student profile. Sapienza, for example, publishes a flat band for students from certain countries, with India in its lowest. Some Master's students also win merit scholarships.

English-taught programmes

Many Master's programmes are taught in English. Most ask for B2-level English, but the exact score is set programme by programme.

Work while you study

A study permit allows part-time work: up to 20 hours a week and 1,040 hours a year. After an Italian degree you can request a 9 to 12 month job-search permit.

How to apply, in order

  1. Choose your course and shortlistWe match your degree, marks and budget to universities and programmes you can verify.
  2. Apply to the universityPolitecnico di Milano's first call for September 2027 entry runs from 1 October to 1 December 2026 for Engineering, and to 28 January 2027 for Architecture and Design. Other universities have their own windows.
  3. Pre-enrol on UniversitalyForeign students register on Universitaly and the university validates it. This comes before the visa application.
  4. Apply for the Type D study visaThrough VFS Global, by appointment. Allow up to 90 days for processing. The consular deadline for 2027/28 Bachelor's and Master's is 31 October 2027, but plan far earlier.
  5. Fly and settle inWe brief you on arrival, accommodation and the residence-permit steps before you travel.

Italian student visa checklist

  • Universitaly pre-enrolment summary
  • Passport valid for at least 3 months beyond the visa period
  • Apostilled and attested degree and transcripts, plus a CIMEA statement or Declaration of Value if your university asks for one
  • English (or Italian) language evidence, usually B2, as the programme requires
  • Proof of accommodation for the first 30 days
  • Six months of bank statements and proof of funds (€10,179.85 in 2026-27)
  • A one-way ticket reservation, not a round trip
  • Health insurance as your consulate requires

Checklists differ slightly between the Italian consulates in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi and Kolkata. We follow the one for your jurisdiction.

Scholarships, work and life after graduation

  • No blanket 100% scholarship exists. Funding comes from several separate routes, each with its own rules and deadlines.
  • Government of Italy (MAECI) scholarship: €10,800 for nine months, for Master's, PhD and research students. It is competitive, and the 2026-27 call closed in March 2026. We will tell you when the next call opens.
  • Regional DSU awards: income- and merit-tested, with deadlines that usually fall in summer. Non-EU students must submit income and asset documents, translated and legalised.
  • University merit scholarships: some universities, Politecnico di Milano for example, offer merit awards to international Master's applicants, including full tuition waivers. They are competitive and have early deadlines.
  • Working: up to 20 hours a week and 1,040 hours a year on a study permit.
  • After you graduate: you can request a 9 to 12 month job-search permit after an Italian degree. We do not promise a job.

Study in Malta

Study in Malta for Indian students

A small island with English-medium degrees, Mediterranean weather and Schengen travel on your doorstep. It also asks you to be careful about which college you choose.

  • Oct & FebUniversity of Malta intakes

Photo: Valletta from Marsamxett Harbour · Martin Furtschegger · CC BY 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Malta covers about 316 square kilometres, yet it has public and private institutions, two main intakes at its university, and a visa process with specific rules.

It suits students who want an English-medium European degree. It also suits families who are willing to check a college's licence before they pay a rupee. We help you do both.

Why students choose Malta

English-medium study

Degrees at the University of Malta and MCAST are taught in English. The University of Malta asks for an approved English test, while MCAST can accept Class 12 English marks or an English-medium letter at some levels.

Schengen on your doorstep

Malta is one of 29 Schengen countries. With a Maltese long-stay visa or residence permit you can travel in other Schengen states for up to 90 days in any 180.

Two intakes at the University of Malta

October and February. Non-EU applicants who need a visa must submit English results by 1 July for October, or 10 November for February.

A tax credit after you graduate

Malta's Get Qualified scheme offers a tax credit of up to 70% of eligible tuition, capped per credit (€75 per ECTS at Bachelor's level, €170 at Master's). It is claimed only against Maltese tax you pay after graduating, so it is not a cash refund.

Where you could study

University of Malta

Msida · Public university

Malta's main public university, founded in 1592, with October and February intakes. Example: BSc IT (Hons) costs non-EU students €10,800 per academic year, and a €1,000 deposit is due within 21 days of your acceptance letter.

MCAST

Paola · Public vocational college

Malta's main vocational college, with programmes from MQF level 1 to 7 in engineering, IT, business, health and creative arts. Applications from outside Malta are generally accepted from April to mid-August.

Licensed private institutions

Various · Private colleges

Several private institutions offer business, hospitality and IT programmes with their own intakes and fees. We only discuss institutions whose licence you can verify on the regulator's database before you pay anything.

Check that a Maltese institution is licensed by the MFHEA, and that your course is full-time and at MQF level 5 or above, before paying any fee. A study permit is granted only for full-time courses at licensed institutions.

How to apply, in order

  1. Choose and verifyWe shortlist courses and check the institution's licence and the course level before anything else.
  2. Apply and sit your English testThe University of Malta needs its English results by 1 July (October intake) or 10 November (February intake) if you need a visa.
  3. Accept the offer and pay the depositThe University of Malta takes €1,000 within 21 days. Get every institution's payment and refund terms in writing first.
  4. Apply for the Type D study visaApply at least 8 weeks, and at most 6 months, before your course starts. The visa application asks for your acceptance letter and proof of payment.
  5. Arrive and apply for your residence permitYou cannot apply for the residence permit from abroad. You apply in Malta after you land.

Maltese student visa and permit checklist

  • Acceptance letter showing the course, its duration and fees, with proof of payment
  • Bank statements for the last three months showing at least 60% of Malta's national equivalised income threshold, pro-rated. A sponsor declaration is allowed.
  • Health insurance of at least €100,000, unless you study at the University of Malta, MCAST or ITS
  • Proof of accommodation, either a lease approved by the Housing Authority or a declaration from your institution
  • Health screening clearance
  • Apostilled and translated documents, and a passport valid for at least 6 months

Identità, Malta's immigration agency, sets these rules and can change them. We check the current requirements for you.

Work, residence and staying safe

  • Working: part-time only. It is stated as up to 20 hours a week, and your employer must obtain an employment licence through Jobsplus. Identità's own pages give different limits, so we confirm the current rule with you.
  • After you graduate: a one-time 9-month post-study permit is available for graduates of MQF level 5 and above. It lets you stay and look for work. It is not a work permit, so a job needs its own permit.
  • Long-term residence needs five years of continuous legal residence. We do not promise residency outcomes.
  • Beware of fake offers. The High Commission of India in Malta warns students to verify admission letters directly with the institution and to be careful about fees that cannot be refunded.
  • Transport: the University of Malta's guide says public transport is free for holders of the e-residence permit.
